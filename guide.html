What this is

A searchable record of every cannabis bill in the Connecticut General Assembly from 2012 to 2026: 231 bills, every committee and floor vote member by member, every amendment and who offered it, and every piece of written testimony posted by the legislature. It also tracks what actually changed in the law, section by section.

Everything links back to the official document on the General Assembly’s website, so you can check any number yourself.

Colors

The same three colors mean the same thing everywhere on the site.

GreenPro-cannabis. For a bill: it expands access or eases penalties. For a person: most of their votes or filings supported that kind of bill, or opposed bills that add penalties.
RedAnti-cannabis. For a bill: it adds penalties, restrictions or enforcement. For a person: most of their votes or filings went that way.
YellowMixed. The bill does both, or the person’s record is split.
GrayRegulation, taxes or a study, with no clear direction. These bills are left out of the pro and anti counts.

A person needs a clear majority, at least two out of three, to get green or red. Each bill’s page shows its statement of purpose, so you can judge the label yourself.

Labels on people who testified

LawmakerState Agency Law EnforcementLocal Official Health and MedicalIndustry Advocate or GroupMember of the Public

Each label comes from the name, title and organization the person filed under. For example, someone filing for a “Department of” is a State Agency, and someone filing for an LLC or a dispensary is Industry. A person with no title or organization is a Member of the Public.

Where a position comes from

Only from the position each witness chose when filing, which the legislature prints in the file name (“Supports” or “Opposes”). Filings from before about 2019 carry no position, so they show as “no side stated”. Nothing is guessed from what the testimony says.

How votes work

Committee voteA committee (like General Law or Judiciary) votes to send a bill forward. Recorded on a tally sheet.
Floor voteThe full House or Senate votes. Recorded on a roll call. A bill must pass both chambers and be signed to become law.
Amendment voteA vote to change a bill before the final vote. These are shown on each record but not counted as pro or anti, because an amendment can help or hurt a bill.
Yea, Nay, AbsentYes, no, or not voting.

Party comes from the state’s official election results for the seat each member held.

Legislative words, in plain English

HB / SBHouse Bill or Senate Bill, followed by its number. Numbers restart every year, so the year matters.
Public Act (P.A.)A bill that passed and became law. “P.A. 21-1” is the first act of 2021.
Joint Favorable (JF)The committee voted to move the bill forward.
Public hearingThe committee session where the public can testify.
Amendment “A”, “B”Amendments offered on the floor, lettered in order. “Adopted” means it passed; “Rejected” means it failed.
LCO numberThe drafting office’s tracking number for a bill version or amendment.
Never calledAn amendment that was filed but never brought up for a vote.
Statement of purposeThe one-paragraph summary printed at the end of every bill.
OLRThe legislature’s Office of Legislative Research, which writes plain-language summaries of bills and amendments.

Where the data comes from

  • Bills, votes, amendments and testimony: the Connecticut General Assembly website (cga.ct.gov).
  • Party: the state’s official election results (electionhistory.ct.gov).
  • What changed in the law: the enacted public acts themselves.
  • Agency actions: Department of Consumer Protection and eRegulations records.
